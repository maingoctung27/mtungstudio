Milano Community
Food Hub

OttantaCinqueJuly 2026 · MilanoPiazzale Selinunte · San Siro

Food is the ultimate universal equalizer. In a neighbourhood divided by invisible economic walls, a Community Food Hub turns the market from a place of transaction into a place of relation — growing, cooking and sharing.

Milano Community Food Hub combines anthropological research and architectural design to create temporary, sustainable community spaces.

Born at the Table

Located at the former covered municipal market in Piazzale Selinunte, the project transforms a disused 968 m² municipal market into a flexible community hub for food-related events, workshops and gatherings.

The shared preparation and consumption of traditional foods becomes a tool for dialogue, knowledge exchange and cultural learning, strengthening social connections across Milan's neighbourhoods.

“The Shared Table” — a community food hub where food is treated not as a commodity, but as a bridge.

Research · San Siro

The Quadrilatero and Piazzale Selinunte form a dense and culturally diverse neighbourhood. The research identifies disconnected green spaces, car-dominated streets, fragmented pedestrian connections and a market separated from the civic life of the piazza.

The number 85 becomes the project's identity: the neighbourhood is described through 85 different nationalities, turning a number associated with fragmentation into a collective identity.

Problem · Solution

The market is trapped between roads, the residential fabric is introverted, and Piazzale Selinunte is disconnected from the market. The project responds by creating a continuous pedestrian landscape, flexible outdoor spaces, a growing corridor and interactive play areas that reconnect the market with everyday neighbourhood life.

Concept

Rituals · Spontaneity · Transition

Between the fixed nodes of the neighbourhood spine, the design creates space for the unprogrammed. Flexible food stalls, shared tables, growing spaces, play areas and outdoor seating allow spontaneous encounters to accumulate into regular community meetings.

The architecture deliberately creates a physical and social “blur” between private and public life, the familiar and unfamiliar.

Design · The Universal Pattern

The project introduces a visual graphic language intended to work across the neighbourhood's many languages and cultures. Pictograms are integrated into the architecture and public realm, while modular elements support a flexible market environment.

Environmental Design

The design responds to Milan's dual climatic challenge: summer overheating and intense rainfall, while also improving winter solar access and protection. Environmental analysis includes sun hours, shadows, UTCI and wind studies.
